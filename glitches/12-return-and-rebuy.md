# Glitch 12 — Return-and-rebuy on price drops 🟡

**Category:** Price protection workaround
**Effort:** Medium — watch prices, do a return
**Risk:** Low. Fully within policy, but heavy use inflates your return ratio.

## How it works

Amazon killed formal post-purchase price adjustment years ago. The modern
workaround: if an item's price drops within your **30-day return window**, you
**return the original** and **rebuy at the lower price**. Net effect = a
price-drop refund.

## How to work it

1. After buying, add the item to a price tracker (**Keepa** /
   **CamelCamelCamel**) and set a drop alert.
2. If the price falls meaningfully while the item is still returnable:
   - If **unopened**: return it, pick return reason "No longer needed" or
     "Other" (avoids defect signals against the product/seller), then rebuy cheap.
   - If **opened but eligible**: still returnable in most categories —
     "Found better price elsewhere" is also an available reason on many returns.
3. Pay attention to **who refunds faster**: refunds to **gift card balance**
   land in hours; card refunds take 3–10 business days.

## The cheeky variant 🟠

Some people skip the physical return: buy cheap → claim "item never arrived"
or "damaged" on the *original* while pocketing the difference. That's the
deceptive version covered in glitches 06/07 — do it sparingly.

## Notes

- Return-and-rebuy is exactly what Rossen Reports (NBC) recommends to
  consumers as the legit play — it is consumer advocacy, not fraud.
- **"Tell Us About a Lower Price"** (feedback link on product pages) can nudge
  Amazon's repricer but guarantees nothing.
